## Paper text (Results; draft for your edit, numbers as above)

**When the LMM wins and when it does not.** Across the sensitivity families the
random-slope LMM held its size near nominal (0.039 to 0.063) under unbalanced
group sizes, residual autocorrelation, curve misspecification, heteroscedastic
residuals and extreme anchor positions, and its power followed total sample size
rather than imbalance. Its measurable advantage was over the per-sample Welch
two-stage test at G = 2, where it gained 0.04 to 0.13 power at Delta/s of 0.5 and
up to 0.30 at 1.0 in unbalanced designs with 2 to 3 samples in a group,
partly because pooling variance across groups buys degrees of freedom at tiny
n. Against the pooled two-stage test (per-sample slopes tested with pooled
variance) the LMM had no measurable advantage in any family: differences were
within 0.003 in unbalanced, heteroscedastic-residual, heteroscedastic-slope and
anchor-extreme designs, and at balanced G = 2 designs at n = 5 and 10 (Stage 2,
within 0.006). The LMM lost to the pooled test only under heavy zero-coverage
dropouts (runs of 5 kb at 2 to 5% of the genome), where its Type I fell to
0.005 to 0.025 and its power fell by up to 0.11 more than the pooled test, with
singular fits in 55 to 84% of datasets; dropouts of 1 kb or at 0.5% had no effect.
Unequal between-sample slope variance (cv ratio 2) inflated Type I of every
pooled-variance test by 0.01 to 0.03 (LMM up to 0.070) and cut power by up to
0.33; none of the arms handled it.

**This draft does not contain the claims in the request** that the LMM wins at
heteroscedastic designs, that it wins in unbalanced designs over the pooled test,
or that it loses at G = 2 balanced designs at larger n. The Stage 3 and Stage 2
data show ties in all three. If those claims are in NOTES.md they are not
supported at these grids; I did not edit NOTES.md.
